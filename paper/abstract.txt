OpenAI's preprint The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths (November 2026) derives its lower bound from a list distinctness theorem: over a finite field, every tensor network that tests whether the $2^w$ records of $2w$ bits in each of $t$ lists are pairwise distinct has volume larger than $t n w^{\sigma}$, where $n=2w\cdot 2^w$ and $\sigma>0$. The theorem is formalized in Lean as BatchDescent.manyList. Read backwards, the circuit to network simulation in the preprint's Proposition 2.3 turns this into a Boolean statement, which the preprint does not state. We state it and prove it in Lean: every fan-in two circuit over the full binary basis that decides element distinctness of $2^w$ strings of $2w$ bits has more than $(n w^{\sigma}-9n-3)/12$ gates, that is, size $\Omega(n(\log n)^{\sigma})$. The proof embeds straight line programs with $s$ gates into the network model with at most $2n+4s$ vertices. We also restate the upstream theorem with all wrappers unfolded and observe that sorting networks force $\sigma\le 1$. Our file builds against the openai/math project (Lean 4.34.1), passes leanchecker, and depends only on the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound; the independent checker nanoda rechecked its full dependency closure of 45,579 declarations without errors. The result is conditional on OpenAI's proof of BatchDescent.hard: the closure draws on 191 upstream modules with about 94,000 lines, of which we have read about 1,000 as human mathematics. The constant $\sigma$ is not explicit, and nothing follows for Turing machine time or for P versus NP. To our knowledge, no superlinear lower bound for general Boolean circuits computing an explicit function was previously known; the best explicit bound we are aware of is $4n-O(\log n)$ (Mishra and Santhanam, ECCC TR26-260).
